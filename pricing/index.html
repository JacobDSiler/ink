Why unlimited subscribers matter

Mailchimp cut their free tier from 2,000 contacts to 500 in 2022. That single change forced tens of thousands of small creators to either downgrade their list, pay $20/month for a tool they weren't using at that scale, or go silent. Ink refuses to make that a business model. Bring 15,000 subscribers over from Mailchimp on your first day, no wall.

What "sends per month" actually means

One send = one email to one recipient. If your list is 5,000 people and you send a Tuesday letter, that's 5,000 sends. The 25k/month free ceiling covers a 5,000-subscriber list emailed weekly, or a 10,000-subscriber list emailed twice a month, or a 25,000-subscriber list emailed once a month. Very few solo authors bump against it. The ones who do have real income to support the $8/month Pro tier.

Scholarship path for creators in real hardship

If you're a creator in circumstances where $8/month is a genuine barrier — a language market with limited earnings, a nonprofit literacy program, or something else the pricing doesn't fit — email folio@jacobsiler.com with a paragraph about your work. Pro or Business is comped for a year, no bureaucracy.

What Ink doesn't do

Ink doesn't try to be Salesforce. No behavioural scoring, no lead-magnet A/B automation, no CRM. If those matter to you, use ActiveCampaign or HubSpot. Ink is for authors who want to write good letters to their readers, on their own schedule, without a monthly SaaS bill they can't justify.
